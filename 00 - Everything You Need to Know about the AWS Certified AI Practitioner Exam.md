The exam is practitioner level, and structured around 5 key domains
- Fundamentals of AI and ML
- Fundamentals of generative AI
- Applications of foundation models
- Guidelines for responsible AI
- Security, compliance, and governance for AI solutions

## Fundamentals of AI and ML
- Make up approximately 20% of the exam
- Covers concepts like basic AI terms, neural networks, natural language processing (NLP), and different learning methods, such as unsupervised, supervised, and reinforcement learning
- Expect questions surrounding the capabilities of AWS's range of AI and ML solutions, such as SageMaker, Amazon Transcribe, Amazon Translate, Amazon Comprehend, Amazon Lex, and Amazon Polly

## Fundamentals of Generative AI
- Make up approximately 25% of the exam
- Be able to explain specific generative AI concepts, including things like prompt engineering, transformer-based models, and the lifecycle of a generative AI model, from pretraining all the way to deployment
- Expect questions about the advantages and disadvantages of generative AI and about what features and solutions AWS offers for developing gen-AI applications
- Expect questions on Amazon SageMaker JumpStart, Amazon Bedrock, PartyRock, and Amazon Q

## Applications of Foundation Models
- Make up the largest section, about 28% of the scored content
- Learn about foundation models, or FMs, going in. FMs are pre-trained models that can be fine-tuned for specific tasks.
- Expect questions around the selection criteria you'd use to choose a pre-trained mdoel, such as cost, latency, complexity, and services you might use

## Guidelines for Responsible AI
- Make up a smaller fraction of the exam at 14%
- Expect questions around responsible AI, such as IS detection, fairness, and the ethical implications of AI

## Security, Compliance, and Governance for AI Solutions
- Make up around 14% of the exam content
- Covers all the AWS solutions you can use to secure those future AI solutions. It covers AIM rules, policies, and permissions, encryption, Amazon Macie, AWS PrivateLink, AWS shared responsibility model, and other related concepts

Historically, AWS exams have had two kinds of questions:
1. Multiple choice - 1 correct, 3 incorrect answers
2. Multiple response - 2 or more correct answers out of five response options

New question types:
1. Ordering - A list of 3 to 5 responses to complete a specific task, and you need to select the correct one and place them in the correct order
2. Matching - A list of responses to match 3 to 7 prompts
3. Case study - Be asked two or more questions about a scenario. Each question is scored individually.
